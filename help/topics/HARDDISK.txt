Topic: HARDDISK
Aliases: INSTALLING CF
Summary: Installing GR-DOS on a hard disk or CF card
Origin: GR-DOS
Group: guide

From a floppy
  Boot from a GR-DOS floppy. FDISK, then FORMAT, erase the hard disk.
  1. Run FDISK. Choose 1, Create DOS Partition, then 1, Create Primary
     DOS Partition, and answer Y to use the whole disk and make it
     active. FDISK restarts the computer; boot the floppy again.
  2. FORMAT C: /S, which also copies IO.SYS, MSDOS.SYS and COMMAND.COM.
  3. Copy the programs you want to a directory, and set PATH to it in
     C:\AUTOEXEC.BAT.
  Then remove the floppy and restart. Like MS-DOS 4.00, GR-DOS uses at
  most 1024 cylinders of a disk and partitions of up to 2 GB.

Examples
  FORMAT C: /S
    Formats C: and makes it bootable.
  MD C:\DOS
    Makes a directory for the programs.
  XCOPY A:\*.* C:\DOS
    Copies the floppy's files to it.

From Linux, on a CF card or disk image
  scripts/deploy-cf in the GR-DOS source sets up a card in a USB reader:
  "provision" once (it erases the card), then "update" for each new
  version. The card gets:
  C:        About 10 MB: the system. Replaced in full at every update.
  C:\DOS    Every program and data file, including the *16 programs
            (HELP DIST16). PATH is C:\DOS.
  D:, E:..  FAT16 drives over the rest of the card, at most 2000 MB
            each. An update never writes to them.

What is yours, and what is replaced
  C:\CONFIG.SYS (FILES=20, BUFFERS=20) and C:\AUTOEXEC.BAT (PROMPT $P$G,
  PATH C:\DOS) are written again at every update, and anything else
  you put on C: is removed. C:\AUTOEXEC.BAT ends by calling
  D:\AUTOEXEC.BAT if there is one: put your own PATH (keep C:\DOS in
  it), KEYB, MODE and the rest there. C:\CONFIG.SYS ends with
  INCLUDEFILE=D:\CONFIG.SYS: put your CONFIG.SYS lines in D:\CONFIG.SYS
  (HELP CONFIG). Updates never change D:\AUTOEXEC.BAT or
  D:\CONFIG.SYS. Keep your files on D: and beyond.

Notes
  A second hard disk with a DOS partition takes D:, and the card's
  drives move up a letter. FDISK, FORMAT and deploy-cf's provision
  erase everything on the disk: copy your files off first.
